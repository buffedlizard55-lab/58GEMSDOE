σ(x) is the distance from a withheld fault segment to the nearest candidate ridge, split into across-strike and along-strike components against the ridge's own normal. A component counts only when ≥ 60 % of its pixels lie within 15 px of a ridge — an unmatched component is a detection failure, not an offset, and is excluded from the scale. Values are pixels (1 px = 100 m); the scoring kernel radius is 3 px. Cross-fitting is leave-one-block-out, so no block contributes to the σ used to emit on itself.

The idealised 1-D companion: a straight fault at across-strike offset δ ~ half-Normal(0, σ) from the detected ridge, every ribbon emitted at the same dot density on the ground (|U|/s fixed, so the FP charge is identical and only TP differs). Under that constraint the tight line wins at every σ, because a dot's along-strike reach (±3 px) is free coverage on a line while an across-strike offset is not. The holdout disagrees, and the reason is segment length: real ridge segments are short, so the stride cannot be raised to pay for extra lines. PROJECTION — model arithmetic, not a measured score.
