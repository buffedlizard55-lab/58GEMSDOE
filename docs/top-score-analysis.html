2. The mechanism, stated as a testable claim

Write the metric as the task brief and the official page give it: DTI = TP / (0.2(TP+FP) + 0.8|G|). Two consequences matter here (both verified by unit tests in tests/test_dti.py):

  1. The denominator's floor 0.8|G| is fixed by the hidden labels. Every unit of predicted mass costs 0.2 whether or not it earns credit. A dot earns credit only if it is the nearest strong mass to a hidden fault pixel within 3 px.
  2. A dot near a known trace earns credit only if hidden labels lie within 300 m of that trace, and it costs FP if they do not. Removing dots near known traces therefore helps exactly when hidden labels are away from known traces.

So the 0.2708 → 0.2778 step (+0.007, user-reported) is consistent with removing FP mass in a band where the hidden labels are absent. The sign of this effect depends on an assumption that we cannot check here: whether new-fault labels lie within 300 m of known traces. A second-hand note (LEARNGEMSDOE PR #3, quoting a forum post) says they may. The forum thread itself (11516) is unreachable from the sandbox. If that note is right, the prune removes true credit and the 0.2778 result is partly luck. Resolve this with an organizer-confirmed receipt, not with a holdout.

4. Can we generate something that scores above 0.2778?

Not demonstrated, and our evidence points the other way for the lane we tested.

  • Our ribbon-lane candidates score pooled holdout DTI ≈ 0.016 and do not beat random placement of the same number of dots (pooled ≈ 0.034). See reports/ribbon_lane_report.json (holdout, baselines).
  • A 0.2778 leaderboard score cannot be compared with our holdout numbers directly. Our holdout is a proxy, and the team's own holdout shows that proxies in this competition are badly miscalibrated (3.); we have no organizer receipts of our own to calibrate against.
  • The three things that could plausibly add score, in order of how well they are supported:
  • Calibrate the holdout against organizer receipts (one or two small submissions inside the weekly cap). Without this, no holdout result in this repo can be turned into a leaderboard projection.
  • Test the prune assumption directly: score base vs. base-with-catalogue-prune on a holdout where withheld segments are placed near (≤ 300 m) and far from visible traces, and see which wins. This decides whether the 0.2778 trick generalises.
  • Improve localisation (σ). Our σ measurement says ridge candidates from the 100 m stack land 5–8 px off faults, no better than random placement (docs/results.html). Finer-scale raw magnetic grids (H1 in docs/hypotheses.md) are the candidate fix, but they are not reachable from this sandbox.
