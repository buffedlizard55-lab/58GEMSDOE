Why did 0.2778 score well, and can we beat it?

What it is. The file gemsdoe32-h33-h33-2-b2-…-zeros.tif is a prune of a 0.2708 base: every dot within 2 px of the catalogue is deleted (37,654 dots, 0 on the catalogue). Its owner's page states "NO ORGANISER SCORE EXISTS" and that the 0.2747 figure is "a MODEL, not a score" (repo).

The GEMSDOE32 owner's own account (their claim, not verified by us). Their page explains why the dotted H19-5 file scored highest with a credit-density argument: each emitted pixel costs 0.2 unless it covers a truth pixel, so thinning a thick surface while keeping its geometry raises credit per pixel. They report an empirical break-even of 0.0548 credit per dot, and say this equals 0.2·0.26 = 0.0520 from the published formula. Those two numbers are not equal (irregularity I-16). Source: GEMSDOE32 index.

Mechanism (plausible, not measured). Organizer staff said pixels of known USGS/INGENIOUS faults are masked from evaluation (forum, 2026-09-16). Masking is pixel-exact, so a dot 1–2 px from a known trace can still fall inside the 300 m kernel of an unmapped fault and score, while a dot 3 px or more away on the catalogue is pure false positive. Removing the catalogue-adjacent band trades a little recall for much less false positive.

Can we beat it from this sandbox? Not with the lane we built. The ribbon lane reached holdout DTI 0.0368. We do not claim a higher score.
