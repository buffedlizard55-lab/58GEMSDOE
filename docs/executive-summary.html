🔬 Method Summary

Hypothesis

Supervised ML (RandomForest) trained on 19-band GeoDAWN geophysical features using existing catalogue faults as positive labels can detect structural lineaments corresponding to unmapped faults across the entire study area.

Approach

ComponentDetail
Features20 gradient features from 10 key GeoDAWN bands (conductivity, topography, gravity, magnetics, seismicity) at 2 Gaussian scales (σ=1.5, σ=3.0)
Training labelsUSGS/INGENIOUS catalogue faults (60,988 pixels) with spatial jitter for robustness
Negative samples292,4457 non-fault pixels (3:1 ratio), excluding 4-pixel buffer around catalogue
ModelRandomForest (100 trees, max_depth=8, sqrt features)
Validation AUC0.7152 (spatial holdout)
Threshold0.45 (ML confidence)
Predictions45,355 binary dots at exactly 1.0, zero on the mapped catalogue

Why this should detect unmapped faults

The DTI metric (α=0.2, β=0.8) rewards coverage 4× more than it penalizes false positives. The competition tests against faults identified by experts but not in the USGS database. By learning the geophysical signature of faults from the19-band feature stack, the model identifies structural discontinuities across the entire study area — including unmapped faults that share similar geophysical expressions.
