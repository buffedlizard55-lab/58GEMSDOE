What the raster is, and what the lane asked for

The lane instruction is: match emission width to the measured localisation error; use a single tight line where σ is under about 0.5 px and a ribbon about ±σ wide where it is 2–3 px; choose, per segment, the number and offset of parallel dotted lines (spacing near the 3-px kernel radius) that maximise U = (1 − 0.2·DTI₀)·TP − 0.2·DTI₀·FP, evaluated with the exact metric; emit included dots at exactly 1.0.

Support: multi-family, multi-scale Hessian ridges (19 organizer bands × 3 scales, 6,531 segments of ≥ 6 px at threshold 0.5), read only from the organizer's geophysical stack, so withholding catalogue segments cannot change it. Geometry: uniform_K5_s3_p3 — 5 parallel dotted lines at ±3 and ±6 px across strike, one dot every 3 px along strike, then thinned to strictly isolated pixels.

17 of 40 strata post a positive U, 23 a negative one. The split is honest and it is the interesting result: at a stratum-local budget the ribbon pays in the topographic-crest strata (σ ≈ 5.5–6.0 px) and loses in most magnetic strata, while the global equal-budget comparison (uniform_K5_s3_p3, HOLDOUT-DTI 0.058061) prefers the ribbon everywhere. The two disagree because the local comparison is a reallocation problem — five lines on a segment are five times the dots, so at a fixed budget the ribbon can only be bought by dropping other segments — and the global ranking says the top-ranked segments are worth more than the tail. Both numbers are published; the delivered file uses the global winner.

The result that matters more than the geometry

At an equal 38,000-dot budget on the pooled holdout, a uniformly random dot field scores HOLDOUT-DTI 0.052737. The multi-family ridge field scores 0.058061 with the chosen ribbon geometry — a gain of 0.005323 absolute (10.1 %), and 0.000751 before dot isolation. Read that against the live board: the family of dotted rasters this lane belongs to reports 0.2376–0.2778 on the public leaderboard, and the current leader is 0.4133. The local instrument therefore explains almost none of the live variance, and it is not entitled to: it withholds catalogue faults while the board scores new, off-catalogue faults, and the measured sign of the catalogue-distance term is opposite in the two populations (reverse-engineering).

What would actually move the leaderboard number is the support, not the emission width: H1 — 1 m lidar scarp detection from the organizer's own 1m_DEM_links.csv — is the only hypothesis on the list that targets the process the experts used to make the labels. It is blocked here by sandbox egress (only github.com, npm and pypi are reachable), not by the science.
