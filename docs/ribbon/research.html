Research: the algebra, the σ table, the sweep

1. The metric, literally

k(d)  = max(1 - d/R, 0),   R = 300 m = 3 px
TP_w  = sum over truth g of  max over predictions x with d(x,g) <= R  of  p(x)*k(d(x,g))
FP_w  = sum over predictions x of  p(x) * (1 - max over truth g of k(d(x,g)))
FN_w  = sum over truth g of  (1 - max over predictions ...)  =  |G| - TP_w
DTI   = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2*FP_w + 0.8*FN_w)  =  TP / (0.2*(TP+FP) + 0.8*|G|)

src/gems58w/metric.py is this transcription; dti_bruteforce is an unoptimised O(|G|·|P|) oracle, and the test suite asserts the fast paths reproduce it to machine precision on random grids.

2. Two corollaries, machine-verified

scripts/ribbon_verify_theorems.py → evidence/theorems.json: all three pass.

  • Denominator identity (FN = |G| − TP): max abs difference 0.0e+00.
  • Binary dominates graded: DTI(λ) strictly increasing on (0, 1] (max abs error vs closed form 5.6e-17). Hence every included dot is written at exactly 1.0.
  • Marginal inclusion: a dot pays iff its kernel credit k ≥ 0.2·DTI₀. Measured break-even bracket 0.0572–0.2546 against a closed-form threshold of 0.0741; every sign agrees.

Two σ numbers are in play, and both are reported. The estimator above uses only detected pixels (≤ 3 px): pooled 1.70 px. The estimator that drives the per-segment plan (`src/gems58w/sigma.py`) is the censored RMS over every pixel of a matched withheld component: median 6.88 px, rms 8.01 px over 994 matched components. σ sits between "a single tight line" (σ < 0.5 px) and the 2–3 px band where the lane prescribes a ribbon — which is exactly why the width question had to be decided by the exact metric, and why the margin is small.
