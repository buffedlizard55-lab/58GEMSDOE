## 2. Why the metric makes exactly that shape win

From `metric.md`, `DTI = TP / (0.2·(TP+FP) + 0.8·|G|)`. Every emitted pixel costs the denominator
exactly `0.2` and returns its kernel credit. Three consequences, each verified in
`evidence/theorems.json`:

* **sparse beats dense** wherever the extra density is not landing on fresh truth;
* **off-catalogue beats on-catalogue**, because the organizer masks the catalogue from both sides —
  a dot on a mapped fault is inert, and it cannot radiate credit;
* **binary beats graded**, so the delivered values are exactly `1.0`.

The group's own evidence disagrees with its own proxies on this point, and the disagreement is the
whole story. On *identical bytes*, a full-catalogue proxy ranks `d1.5 = 0.17193 > h19-5 = 0.16635 >
d2.8 = 0.16177` — the exact opposite of the reported live order. A proxy that keeps the catalogue in
scoring rewards dots placed on the catalogue. The competition deletes those pixels.
